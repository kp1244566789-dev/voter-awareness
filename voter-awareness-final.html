Why every vote counts

In a democracy, citizens choose the government. Low turnout means decisions are made with fewer voices, which is why awareness is the first step.

  • Your vote is your voice. Laws, schools, roads and jobs policy are decided by the people you elect.
  • Small margins are real. In the 2008 Rajasthan Assembly election, a candidate in the Nathdwara seat lost by a single vote.
  • Young voters are a big group. Many first-time voters never register, so awareness and easy online registration matter.
  • Not voting hands the decision to others. Even choosing NOTA is a way of taking part.

For verified turnout figures, see the section Voting in India.

How to become a voter

  1. Check eligibility: You must be an Indian citizen aged 18 or above.
  2. Apply online: Fill Form 6 on voters.eci.gov.in or in the ECINET app.
  3. Upload documents: Photo, proof of age and proof of address.
  4. Track your status: Enter your reference number in the app or portal.
  5. Check the voter list: Confirm your name is on the electoral roll before polling day.

The role of digital platforms

ECINET app

Launched in January 2026, it brings many Election Commission services into one app: registration, name search, application tracking and e-EPIC download.

Social media

Short videos and posters on Instagram, YouTube and X reach young voters fast.

SVEEP campaigns

The Election Commission's voter education programme, using both digital and on-ground outreach.

cVIGIL

Report model code of conduct violations with a photo or video. It is now available inside ECINET.

Helpline 1950

Phone support for voter-related questions.

WhatsApp and SMS

Reminders and local-language information delivered directly.

Voting in India

India is called the world's largest democracy. Every citizen aged 18 or above has the right to vote, whatever their religion, caste, gender or income. This is guaranteed by Article 326 of the Constitution as universal adult suffrage.

National Voters' Day

Celebrated on 25 January, the day the Election Commission of India was founded in 1950, to encourage new voters.

Voting age 18

The voting age was lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Constitutional Amendment, which brought millions of young people into elections.

Your voter ID (EPIC)

The Elector Photo Identity Card is the main document used to prove who you are at the polling booth. You can also download a digital copy, the e-EPIC, from ECINET.

Source: Election Commission of India, Lok Sabha 2024 statistics.

How you vote: EVM, VVPAT and NOTA

EVM

The Electronic Voting Machine has a Ballot Unit, where you press the blue button next to your candidate, and a Control Unit with the polling officer.

Beep and red light

A long beep and a red light next to your choice show that your vote has been recorded.

VVPAT

The Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail machine shows a paper slip with your choice for about 7 seconds before it drops into a sealed box.

NOTA

"None of the Above" is the last option on the machine. It lets you take part in the election without choosing any candidate.

Your polling day, step by step

  1. Find your booth: Check your polling station on the ECINET app or voters.eci.gov.in.
  2. Carry your ID: Take your voter ID (EPIC) or another accepted photo ID.
  3. Join the queue: Polling usually runs through the day, and voters in line before closing time are allowed to vote.
  4. Verification: An officer checks your name on the voter list and your ID.
  5. Indelible ink: The ink mark on your left forefinger shows that you have voted.
  6. Cast your vote: Press the button for your choice and check the VVPAT slip.
  7. Step out proudly: You have just taken part in the world's largest election. Share the message.

Special Intensive Revision (SIR)

SIR is a detailed exercise in which the Election Commission revises the electoral roll through house-to-house visits by Booth Level Officers (BLOs) and fresh verification. Its second phase began in late 2025 in nine states and three Union Territories, and in some states a large number of names were removed from the rolls. Check whether it applies in your state on eci.gov.in.

Challenges and solutions

Fake news

Problem: false messages about voting spread quickly. Solution: verify information only on official sources like eci.gov.in.

Digital divide

Problem: not everyone has a smartphone or good internet. Solution: pair online campaigns with helpline 1950 and on-ground outreach.

Privacy

Problem: scam links ask for personal details. Solution: use only the official app and portal, and never share OTPs.

Short attention

Problem: long posts get ignored. Solution: use short videos, clear posters and local languages.

Elections for everyone

Persons with disabilities

Polling stations offer ramps and wheelchair help. PwD voters can request facilities through the ECI app (Saksham is now part of ECINET).

Senior citizens

In the 2024 Lok Sabha elections, voters aged 85 and above and voters with benchmark disabilities could choose to vote from home.

Women voters

Women turned out in slightly higher numbers than men in 2024. Some booths are run entirely by women staff.

Third gender voters

Turnout among transgender electors rose from 14.64% in 2019 to 27.09% in 2024, and registration grew too.

First-time voters

Turning 18 is a milestone. Register early and check your name before every election.

Overseas Indians

Citizens living abroad can register as overseas electors. About 1.19 lakh were registered in 2024.

Myths and facts

Myth: My single vote will not change anything.

Fact: Elections are the sum of individual votes, and some seats are decided by very small margins.

Myth: I must visit an office to register.

Fact: You can apply online through the voter portal or the ECINET app.

Myth: Anything forwarded on WhatsApp about elections is true.

Fact: Always cross-check dates and rules on official Election Commission channels.

Myth: Voter awareness is only for young people.

Fact: First-time voters need guidance, but updating addresses and checking names matters for every age group.

Frequently asked questions

My name is not on the voter list. What should I do?

Fill Form 6 on voters.eci.gov.in or in the ECINET app. You can also search the list there to see whether your name is already included.

What is SIR and what should I do?

SIR is the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. Check your name on ECINET or voters.eci.gov.in, keep your documents ready, and respond when your BLO visits.

What is ECINET?

ECINET is the Election Commission's platform and app, launched in January 2026. It offers voter forms, name search, application tracking and e-EPIC download.

I have moved. How do I update my address?

Use Form 8 on the portal or in the app to change your address.

Does one vote really matter?

Yes. Many seats have been decided by small margins, and every vote adds up to the result.

Which ID can I use to vote?

The voter ID card (EPIC) is the main one. The Election Commission also accepts some other photo IDs; the list is on eci.gov.in.

Conclusion

Digital platforms make voter awareness faster, cheaper and more interactive. Combined with trusted official sources and on-ground outreach, they can help more eligible citizens register, check their names and turn up to vote. Awareness is the first step, and voting is the one that counts.
